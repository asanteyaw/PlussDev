# Biology

Biological models describe how quantities such as population sizes, concentrations and forces change in time. The guides begin with populations: how a single species grows and how its growth is limited.

1. **How does a population grow?** Exponential, logistic and Gompertz growth, with closed forms to check a numerical integration against and batches of growth rates solved together.

2. **How do species affect each other?** Predators and prey, and communities of competing species.

3. **What is the future of a population?** Stage matrices, lifetimes, sensitivities and the chance of extinction.

4. **What can a metabolic network do?** Flux balance, flux variability and knockouts.
5. **How fast does an enzyme work?** Rate laws with inhibitors, and progress curves.

6. **How far does an epidemic go?** The SIR model, its closed-form outcome and contact rates.
7. **How does a drug move through the body?** Compartment models, exposure and repeated dosing.

8. **How do genes change in a population?** Selection and drift.
9. **How does a neuron fire?** The leaky neuron and firing patterns.
10. **What does a sequence say about a protein?** Charge and pH.

11. **How does a muscle produce force?** Force curves, and the balance between fibers and tendon.

12. **How does diffusion create patterns?** Turing instability.
13. **How does blood flow?** Resistance, pulsation, wave speed and the Windkessel.
